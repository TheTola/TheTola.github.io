It’s been a minute since our Kingdom Date, and I wanted to give you a few words of encouragement. This is a rare opportunity to hear someone’s first impressions of you from a stranger who genuinely means well.

From what you told me, I think you have such an interesting family and upbringing. I really hope and pray that your father eventually becomes a disciple. I can also tell that you have a very big heart for other people, and I get the impression that you’re a really big family person. I think being able to bring that kind of heart into the Kingdom is awesome.

There’s one thing in particular that I wanted to leave you with. I don’t remember exactly what made me keep thinking about this, but I think you mentioned that you tend to be hard on yourself.

That’s something I feel like I’ve seen in a lot of people in the Kingdom. Sometimes it comes from having a difficult past, and sometimes people just naturally have a tendency to beat themselves up over what they did, what they should have done, what they aren’t doing well enough, or who they think they should already be.

But something I think is important to remember is that, from the very beginning, mankind messed things up. God made man, man sinned, and then God spent millennia preparing a way for mankind to come back to Him.

And that way was Jesus.

Jesus came down, humbled Himself, became the least of men, suffered, and died so that we could be reconciled to God. I don’t know what kind of divine physics made that the best way for God to reconcile us to Himself, but I think the point is important:

It was never about us. It was for us.

He gave us a path back to Him, but the focus is still Him—not us.

Sometimes life starts life-ing, and without realizing it, we become completely focused on ourselves: our failures, our struggles, our weaknesses, our past, what we should be doing better, and everything we think is wrong with us.

But Scripture is filled with God using people and things that nobody would have expected.

God used ravens to feed His prophet. He used poor and seemingly insignificant people to confront kings. He used murderers to help bring people into His Kingdom. God has never been limited by the person He chooses to work through.

So it is not ultimately about what you did, what you do, what you have not done, or whether you feel like you’re enough.

It’s about what He can do with you if you let Him.

I think sometimes we spend so much time looking at ourselves that we forget to look at the God who is actually doing the work.

Anyway, I hope I get the chance to know you better in the future. From the little I’ve gotten to know about you so far, I think you have a lot that you can bring to the Kingdom.
