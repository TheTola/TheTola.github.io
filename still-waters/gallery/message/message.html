Dear Serenity McCarty,

Hi Serenity,

How’s it going? It has been a week since our E-Date, and I wanted to send you this letter of encouragement. It was really awesome getting to talk to you.

Something I thought of recently is connected to John 15:1–2. A long time ago, I read a book that shared a story about how the author, who was a pastor, had his understanding of this verse changed after he purchased a vineyard. Once he had a vineyard and started working it himself, he began to realize so many things about that verse that, as a non-farmer, he never would have thought of before.

One of those things was pruning. People often think of pruning as simply cutting off a leaf or part of the plant, but he said he discovered that pruning was actually a multi-step process. The first step of pruning was not always to cut something off and throw it away. Sometimes you do cut it, and graft it somewhere else, lift it up, and place it in a better part of the vine.

For instance, if a branch is on the ground, touching the floor and covered in soil, the farmer might come, clean it off, and place it somewhere else—somewhere it can now receive more sunlight, more nutrients, and no longer be covered in dirt.

You see, when the branch is at the bottom and covered in soil—it cannot receive sunlight properly. No matter how much sunlight is available, the soil blocks it.

So the farmer, wanting the branch to bear more fruit, recognizes that the branch is not useless. It is simply being hampered by its environment. So he lifts it up, cleans it off, and places it in a better location. This allows the branch to receive the sunlight it needs.

I still agree with that author. Oftentimes, we see hardships in our lives as punishment, and we start looking for someone to blame. Sometimes we blame ourselves. Sometimes we blame the people around us. But oftentimes, it is really nothing more than God moving us from one place to another.

That was one of the things I kept thinking about after our conversation. I do not know everything you have walked through, but I do know that sometimes God’s care does not always feel gentle in the moment. Sometimes it feels like being moved, interrupted, or unsettled. But even then, He may be placing us somewhere we can receive what we need and bear more fruit.

I wanted to share that with you because our conversation stayed on my mind. I can tell that you have an awesome, loving heart, and I hope I get more chances to get to know you in the future.

-Oluwatola Ayedun
