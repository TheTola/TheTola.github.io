Dear Amanda Miller,

Hello Amanda, i've been meaning to write this for some time, but I ran into a very significant set of issues. First, I made some changes to the app I used to create this letter, and in the process I broke it. It took quite a while to get everything working again. But the more significant issue wasn't fixing the app—it was figuring out what to say.

In situations like this, words never seem adequate. The most common thing people say is, "I'm sorry for your loss." While those words come from a place of compassion, I found myself feeling that they simply remind someone of the pain they're already carrying.

so after contemplating i came up with encouragement a reminder that while life has downs it also has ups and that joy DOES come in the morning a reminder that while life has its valleys, it also has its mountaintops. Seasons of sorrow are real, but so are seasons of healing. And even when the night feels unbearably long, joy does come in the morning.

"For his anger lasts only a moment, but his favor lasts a lifetime; weeping may stay for the night, but rejoicing comes in the morning."
— Psalm 30:5
